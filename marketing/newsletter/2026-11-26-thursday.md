**Subject:** She said no to a job at $1.5 million homes
**Preview text:** Why walking away from underpriced work is a business skill

---

Hi [First name, or "Hi there"],

In Sydney, Australia, a cleaning business owner called Sara Allan runs a company named Housekeeping Heroes.

She was offered a big job: a pre-handover clean of brand-new townhouses, each worth around **$1.5 million**. Most people would grab it. Big homes, big name on your portfolio.

Sara said no.

Her reason was simple. The pay offered was far too low for the work. And with new homes, when a buyer finds a problem, builders are quick to blame the cleaner. So she would be underpaid *and* carry the risk.

(This is her side of the story. The builder's response wasn't reported.)

Here's the lesson for us: **a fancy house does not mean a good job.** The size of the client doesn't pay your staff. Your price does.

Before you accept any job, you need to know one number: **your floor price.** That's the lowest price where you still cover:
- staff pay and feeding
- transport
- materials and equipment wear
- a share of your overhead
- and at least some profit

Below that number, you're not "getting a client". You're paying to work.

Once you know your floor price, saying no stops feeling scary. It becomes a calculation, not an emotion.
