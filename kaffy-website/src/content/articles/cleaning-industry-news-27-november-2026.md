## Lagos calls off this Saturday's sanitation exercise

![A street cleaner sweeping a market street (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-27/street-sweeper.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Chuot Anhls on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/man-cleaning-the-street-in-city-18792049/)*

**What happened:** Lagos State has cancelled the monthly environmental sanitation exercise that was due to hold on Saturday, 28 November. Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu ordered the cancellation so that it would not clash with INEC's nationwide mock accreditation exercise, which starts at 8.30am the same morning. The state had earlier said the exercise would go ahead as planned, before the governor's office reversed that position on Friday.

**My take:** If you run a cleaning business in Lagos, this changes your Saturday. Many of us block out the sanitation morning, so check your bookings now and let clients know you can come earlier than usual. More importantly, see how quickly the plan changed: announced, confirmed, then cancelled within two days. Build a habit of checking official announcements the evening before, and keep a simple message ready to send to clients whenever plans change.

Read the full story: [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/928408-lagos-cancels-november-environmental-sanitation-exercise-over-inec-mock-accreditation.html)

## Cleaners and catering staff at a London school plan to strike over pay

![An empty school corridor with a shiny floor (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-27/school-hallway.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Caleb Oquendo on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/bright-empty-school-hallway-with-bulletin-boards-35758714/)*

**What happened:** Cleaners and catering staff at a private school in Walthamstow, east London, plan to strike from Monday 30 November until Sunday 13 December over what they describe as a pay cut. The workers are members of the union Unite, which says the action will affect the school's Christmas events and leave areas uncleaned. These are the union's claims as reported by the Waltham Forest Echo.

**My take:** When cleaners stop working, everybody notices. Clean is invisible until it is missing, and that is exactly why your staff deserve respect. If you ever need to change pay or hours, talk to your team early, explain the reasons honestly and put it in writing. A surprise cut destroys trust faster than anything else. And for those of us who clean schools, timing matters: never let a staffing problem land just before the busiest week of a client's year.

Read the full story: [Waltham Forest Echo](https://walthamforestecho.co.uk/2026/11/27/catering-staff-and-cleaners-at-walthamstow-private-school-strike-over-disgraceful-pay-cut)

## Hundreds of domestic workers in Chennai call for fair pay and safety

![Hands washing a white plate with a sponge at a kitchen sink (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-27/washing-dishes.webp)
*Illustrative photo: cottonbro studio on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/cleaning-of-white-ceramic-plate-5904033/)*

**What happened:** More than 500 domestic workers gathered in Chennai, India, on 25 November to launch a 16-day campaign against gender-based violence, organised by the Tamil Nadu Domestic Workers Welfare Trust. A Tamil Nadu state minister who spoke at the event called for fair wages and protections for domestic workers.

**My take:** Domestic workers in India, Nigeria and across Africa face the same problems: low pay, long hours and no protection when something goes wrong in a client's home. As cleaning business owners, we can do better than the informal market. Give every cleaner a written agreement, a clear way to report a problem with a client, and the right to leave a job where they feel unsafe. That is not just the right thing to do; it is how you attract and keep the best people.

Read the full story: [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/domestic-workers-call-for-fair-wages-safety-and-dignity-at-chennai-campaign-launch)

## The first US apprenticeship for cleaners starts next week

![Yellow-gloved hands holding a sponge and a spray bottle (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-27/cleaning-supplies-gloves.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Jonathan Borba on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/close-up-of-cleaning-supplies-with-gloves-28576636/)*

**What happened:** Earlier this month, the US Department of Labor approved ISSA, the global cleaning industry association, as the first sponsor of a registered apprenticeship for Cleaning Technicians. It is a one-year programme that combines structured training with work on the job, with a focus on safety and infection prevention. The first group of about 20 apprentices is due to start on 1 December.

**My take:** This is a big step for respect in our industry. Cleaning is a skilled trade, and now it has a formal training path like plumbing or electrical work. You don't need a government programme to do the same in your business. Write down how you want every task done, train new staff against that checklist, pair them with an experienced cleaner for their first weeks, and give them a certificate when they pass. Trained cleaners work faster, break fewer things and stay longer.

Read the full announcement: [ISSA](https://www.issa.com/articles/issa-approved-by-us-department-of-labor-as-first-sponsor-of-cleaning-technician-registered-apprenticeship/)
