Here is what happened in the cleaning industry this week, and what I think it means for those of us running cleaning businesses. Click the links to read each full story.

## 1. A Toronto cleaning company opened a second office, closer to its clients

**What happened:** Now It's Clean, a residential and commercial cleaning company, has opened a downtown Toronto office on Peter Street. It joins the company's existing office in North York. The company says the new base puts it closer to condo residents, homeowners and businesses in the city centre. It has served Toronto for more than five years.

**My take:** Look at the reason they gave: *closer to the clients*. In cleaning, distance costs money. Every extra kilometre is transport, time and tired staff. You don't need a second office to apply this. Look at where most of your paying clients live and work, and try to group your jobs by area on the same day. Less time on the road means more jobs, and more profit from each one.

## 2. A UK cleaning company is training its cleaners with AI, in their own language

**What happened:** Peartree Cleaning, a UK commercial cleaning company, is rolling out AI-powered training for its frontline staff. Its written "toolbox talks" are being turned into short video briefings with an AI presenter, available in many languages. Many of its cleaners don't speak English as a first language. The company's point: asking someone to read a document and sign it doesn't mean they've understood it. Managers can track who has completed each module, and the full programme should be in place by 1 January 2027.

**My take:** You don't need AI to learn from this. If your cleaners are more comfortable in Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo or Pidgin, train them in that language. A two-minute voice note or video on WhatsApp showing *how* to clean a bathroom properly will do more than a long written list nobody reads. Then check that they've understood. Ask them to show you, not just say "yes ma".

Read the full story: [FMJ](https://www.fmj.co.uk/peartree-rolls-out-ai-powered-multilingual-training/)

## 3. A big cleaning chemical supplier bought another company's chemical business

**What happened:** Betco, an American maker of professional cleaning chemicals, has bought the chemical business of Fuller Industries. Production is moving from Fuller's plant in Great Bend, Kansas, to Betco's plant in Ohio. Fuller's workers have been offered the chance to apply for jobs at Betco if they relocate, but some local workers are losing their jobs. Fuller sold its brush business separately to another company.

**My take:** Supplier deals like this happen far from us, but the lesson travels: **don't depend on one supplier.** When a supplier is sold, prices, products and delivery can change overnight. Know at least two places where you can buy your key chemicals and equipment, and keep a small stock so one bad week doesn't stop your jobs.

Read the full story: [CleanLink](https://www.cleanlink.com/news/article/Betco-Acquires-Fuller-Industries-Chemical-Assets--33205)

## 4. Are layoffs coming for your clients?

**What happened:** Cleaning Business Today warns home cleaning companies that white-collar layoffs, which many companies are blaming on AI, could hit demand for home cleaning. Its argument: the households most at risk are professional and tech-sector families, which are also the households most likely to hire cleaners. Home cleaning is something people cut when money gets tight. Not everyone agrees that AI is really behind the layoffs, but the risk to cleaning demand is the same either way.

**My take:** This is about the US, but every economy has its own version: salary delays, job cuts, prices going up. If most of your income comes from one type of client, you are exposed. Spread your risk. Mix residential clients with a few commercial contracts (offices, schools, churches, estates), because businesses keep cleaning even when households cut back.

Read the full story: [Cleaning Business Today](https://cleaningbusinesstoday.com/blog/ai-layoffs-home-cleaning-business/)

## 5. Refill concentrates are growing fast

**What happened:** Neat, a UK brand that sells cleaning products as concentrated refills you mix with water in a reusable bottle, says it has now sold more than six million refills and has opened its own manufacturing facility. Other brands are launching refill concentrates for the Christmas season too.

**My take:** Concentrates aren't just "eco". They are a cost lesson. When you buy ready-to-use liquids, you are paying to transport water. Buying concentrates and diluting them correctly in labelled bottles can cut your materials cost per job. Just measure properly: too strong wastes money and can damage surfaces, too weak doesn't clean.

Read the full story: [Retail Times](https://retailtimes.co.uk/neat-is-on-a-mission-to-make-concentrated-refills-the-new-standard/)

## 6. A cleaning company is giving away a "how to start a cleaning business" guide

**What happened:** Right Touch Cleaning Services in Niagara Falls, Canada, has published a step-by-step ebook on how to start a cleaning business, written by its Director of Operations, alongside a free starter guide.

**My take:** I love this, because it's exactly what I believe in. When you teach what you know, people trust you. You don't have to write an ebook. Share one useful tip a week on your WhatsApp status or Instagram: how to remove a stain, how to keep a kitchen clean between visits. Clients hire the cleaner who already helped them for free.

Read more: [openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4699130/niagara-falls-cleaning-company-publishes-how-to-start)

## Quick tip of the week

December is the busiest month for many cleaning businesses: end-of-year deep cleans, office closings and Christmas guests. Price those jobs properly *now*, before the rush. Count your real costs (staff, feeding, transport, materials, equipment wear and overhead) and add your profit on top. Busy and underpaid is still underpaid.
