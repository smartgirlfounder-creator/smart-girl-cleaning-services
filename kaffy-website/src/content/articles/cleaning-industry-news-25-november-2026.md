## Robot vacuums are heavily discounted for Black Friday

![A robot vacuum on a wooden floor next to a sofa (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-25/robot-vacuum-living-room.webp)
*Illustrative photo: cottonbro studio on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/man-smartphone-house-technology-6856831/)*

**What happened:** Black Friday falls on 27 November this year, and robot vacuum makers have already started their sales. Tech sites report that Roborock is offering up to half off selected models, and eufy says its robot vacuum discounts run from 19 to 30 November. Amazon's sale also includes several big robot vacuum brands.

**My take:** Every year more homes buy a robot that does the daily floor sweep, and some cleaning business owners panic about it. Don't. A robot vacuum keeps the floor tidy between visits, but it doesn't scrub a shower, wipe skirting boards, clean an oven or change bed linen. Stop selling "I will sweep and mop" and start selling the deep, detailed work a robot can't do. And if a client already owns one, offer to empty, clean and maintain it as part of your visit.

Read the full story: [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/roborock-black-friday-2026-deals-3736129/)

## Lagos governor plans to restructure LAWMA

![A pile of black rubbish bags on a street (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-25/rubbish-bags.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Tope J. Asokere on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/a-pile-of-trash-bags-5789369/)*

**What happened:** Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu said he will restructure the Lagos Waste Management Authority (LAWMA) after weeks of complaints about refuse piling up on roads, streets and drains. He said more staff should be "on the streets and on the road, not in air-conditioned offices", and that more environmental officers will be deployed to monitor waste collection. He also questioned whether all of the state's private waste operators are performing.

**My take:** Notice what the government is really saying: it wants people who deliver on the ground, and it is reviewing operators who don't. That is the same standard every client will hold you to. If you run a cleaning business in Lagos, think about where waste fits in your services: proper bagging, sorting and disposal for offices and estates, and a clear plan for what happens to the rubbish after you clean. Clients will pay for a business that solves the whole problem, not half of it.

Read the full story: [The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2914122)

## A sewer cleaner in India now operates a cleaning robot

![A worn metal manhole cover (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-25/manhole-cover.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Anton Ivanov on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/top-view-of-a-manhole-cover-23482502/)*

**What happened:** India's Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment shared the story of a 41-year-old worker from Mysuru, Karnataka, who spent 15 years cleaning sewers and septic tanks by hand. After training under the government's NAMASTE scheme, he now operates a robotic system that cleans manholes, so he no longer has to go down into the sewer himself. The story comes from a government release, as reported by Indian Masterminds.

**My take:** This is the right way to think about machines in our industry. The robot didn't take this man's job; it took away the most dangerous part of it and made him a skilled operator. For your own business, ask: which tasks put my cleaners at risk, and what tool or training would make them safer? Proper gloves, masks, long-handled tools and safety training are not extras. They protect your people and they show clients you run a professional business.

Read the full story: [Indian Masterminds](https://indianmasterminds.com/news/namaste-scheme-sanitation-worker-robotic-training-mysuru-252939)

## Saudi Arabia and Kenya agree to cut domestic worker recruitment costs

![A woman walking along a country path carrying a suitcase (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-25/traveller-with-suitcase.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Ömer Derinyar on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/back-view-of-a-woman-walking-with-a-suitcase-on-a-field-18399016/)*

**What happened:** On 12 November, Saudi Arabia and Kenya signed a technical annex to their July 2026 agreement on recruiting domestic workers. It aims to simplify the process and reduce the cost of hiring Kenyan domestic workers in Saudi Arabia. The two countries also agreed a three-month pilot to review recruitment fees. Saudi Arabia's labour ministry said the annex covers domestic workers only, not skilled workers.

**My take:** Many cleaners across Africa, including Nigeria, are being recruited to work in homes abroad, and that affects who is available to work for us at home. If good cleaners keep leaving, it is partly because they see better pay and status elsewhere. Make your business a place worth staying: pay on time, train people properly, give them a uniform and a title, and show them a path to becoming a supervisor. People stay where they feel valued and see a future.

Read the full story: [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/666211/saudi-arabia/saudi-arabia-kenya-sign-deal-to-regulate-recruitment-of-domestic-workers)

## Washington State's ban on "forever chemicals" in cleaning products starts in January

![Bottles of cleaning products on a metal shelf (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-25/cleaning-products-shelf.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Ron Lach on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/bottles-of-cleaning-products-standing-on-metal-shelf-10558189/)*

**What happened:** From 1 January 2027, Washington State in the US will restrict cleaning products that have PFAS, often called "forever chemicals", intentionally added to them. The rule from the state's Department of Ecology also covers clothing and car washes, and products made before the deadline can still be sold. Maine and Colorado already restrict PFAS in cleaning products from 2026.

**My take:** Even if you don't work in America, pay attention, because the products we buy are made by global companies and the rules in big markets change what ends up on our shelves. Clients are also asking more questions about what goes into their homes, especially families with babies and pets. Know what is in the products you use, keep the labels and safety sheets, and be ready to explain your choices. "I know exactly what I use in your home" is a powerful thing to be able to say.

Read more: [Washington State Department of Ecology](https://ecology.wa.gov/spwa-pfas)

## Quick tip of the week

Christmas, New Year and end-of-year parties are coming in Nigeria and everywhere else. Put together a simple "before the guests arrive" package and a "morning after the party" package now, price them clearly, and start telling your clients this week.
