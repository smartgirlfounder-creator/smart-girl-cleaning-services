## Chicago's hotel strike has spread to 11 hotels

![An empty, softly lit hotel corridor (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-22/hotel-corridor.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Rafael Rodrigues on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/long-hotel-hallway-with-modern-lighting-34728173/)*

**What happened:** On 14 November, union workers at three more Chicago hotels, the Royal Sonesta, the Marriott Marquis and The Wade Hotel, walked off the job, bringing the number of hotels on strike in the city to 11. It is part of a wider contract fight involving more than 7,000 workers at 46 hotels, whose contracts expired on 31 August. Workers, including housekeepers, are asking for better pay, health care, pensions and safer workloads. The hotels say they are already paying more than ever in wages and that rising labour costs are a top concern.

**My take:** Look at what the workers are asking for: not just money, but safer workloads. Housekeepers everywhere know the pain of being given too many rooms in too little time. In your own business, be honest about how long a job really takes, and price it so your cleaners are not rushing and burning out. A tired, overworked cleaner makes mistakes, and those mistakes cost you clients. Fair workloads are good for your team and good for your quality.

Read the full story: [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/news/2026/11/15/workers-at-3-more-hotels-join-strike-for-better-wages-benefits)

## Lagos is training its sanitation workers on safety

![A worker sweeping a narrow street with a broom (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-22/street-sweeper.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Chuot Anhls on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/man-cleaning-the-street-in-city-18792049/)*

**What happened:** The Lagos State Government held a one-day workshop for sanitation workers in mid-November, covering occupational safety, safe waste handling and the use of protective equipment. Officials said the aim is to improve the workers' competence, welfare and service delivery, and workers were urged to assess risks before starting any task.

**My take:** I love seeing this, because safety training is something many small cleaning businesses in Nigeria skip. It doesn't have to be expensive. Before you send anyone to a job, show them how to use gloves and other protective equipment, which chemicals must never be mixed, and how to lift heavy things safely. An injured cleaner is a human cost first, and a business cost too. Train your team, write it down, and tell your clients you do it.

Read the full story: [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/11/lagos-retrains-sanitation-workers-on-safety-efficiency/)

## A US janitorial company has been sold to new investors

![Window cleaners working on the glass front of a tall office building (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-22/window-cleaners-glass-building.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Aleksandar Pasaric on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/five-person-cleaning-glass-building-625279/)*

**What happened:** Silver Oak Services Partners has sold CCS Facility Services, a US provider of janitorial, engineering and specialty facility services, to the investment firm Wind Point Partners. The sale was announced on 13 November and the price was not disclosed. Under Silver Oak, CCS grew partly by buying other cleaning companies in different US regions.

**My take:** Investors keep buying commercial cleaning companies because steady contracts bring steady income. That is a lesson for us, even at a much smaller size. A business with signed contracts, regular clients, clean records and systems that run without you is a business someone may one day want to buy. Build your company as if you might sell it one day, even if you never do.

Read more: [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/experience/troutman-pepper-locke-advises-silver-oak-and-ccs-facility-services-in-sale-of-ccs-to-wind-point-partners/)

## Another floor-cleaning robot was launched at the ISSA Show

![A robot vacuum cleaner on a wooden floor (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-22/robot-vacuum-floor.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Jens Mahnke on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/round-robot-vacuum-844874/)*

**What happened:** VIGGO launched the Mix1Pro, a commercial cleaning robot, at the ISSA Show North America in Las Vegas, which ran from 17 to 19 November. The company says it is designed for compact, complex indoor spaces of up to about 53,800 square feet, with improvements in navigation, cleaning and automated servicing.

**My take:** Robots are coming for large, open floors first: shopping centres, offices and warehouses. That still leaves plenty for people to do, from detail cleaning to the corners, the toilets and the client relationship. If you serve commercial clients, start learning about these machines now, so that when a client asks, you can advise them instead of losing the contract to someone who can.

Read the full announcement: [VIGGO on PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/viggo-launches-mix1pro-commercial-cleaning-robot-at-issa-show-north-america-2026-302946803.html)
