## Airbnb guests no longer have to do checkout chores

![Hands arranging fresh bed linen (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-24/making-bed-linen.webp)
*Illustrative photo: cottonbro studio on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/person-holding-white-textile-on-bed-4108801/)*

**What happened:** Airbnb has changed its rules so hosts can no longer require guests to do time-consuming checkout chores that a professional cleaner would normally handle, such as stripping beds or washing dishes. Hosts can still ask for light tasks, like leaving dishes in the sink. The new policy applies to reservations booked on or after 20 November 2026.

**My take:** This is good news for short-let cleaners. If guests aren't doing the work, the host needs someone who will, and that someone can be you. If you clean shortlets in Lagos, Abuja or anywhere else, go back to your host clients and offer a full turnover package: linen change, dishes, rubbish out, restock. Price it per turnover so the host knows exactly what each booking costs them.

Read the full story: [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/10/21/airbnbs-new-policy-for-hosts-dont-ask-guests-to-wash-dishes-or-strip-beds/)

## Nigeria signs a revised order to end open defecation by 2030

![A clean white toilet in a bathroom (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-24/white-toilet.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Jonas Thomann on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/close-up-of-modern-white-toilet-in-wc-7545637/)*

**What happened:** Vice President Kashim Shettima announced that President Bola Tinubu has signed a revised Executive Order 009 on an Open Defecation-Free Nigeria, which is now being gazetted. The revision follows the move of the national target from 2025 to 2030, and officials say it will strengthen monitoring and accountability and bring in more private sector participation.

**My take:** "Private sector participation" is the phrase I want every Nigerian cleaning business owner to notice. Public toilets need to be built, but they also need to be cleaned and maintained every single day, and that is where many projects fail. If you want government or NGO contracts, start now: get your business registered, keep proper records, and put together a simple proposal for maintaining public or school toilets in your area.

Read the full story: [The Sun Nigeria](https://thesun.ng/shettima-signs-revised-order-to-end-open-defecation/)

## A UK cleaning firm wins a Manchester office contract

![An empty open-plan office with rows of desks (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-24/empty-office.webp)
*Illustrative photo: cottonbro studio on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/an-empty-office-5483236/)*

**What happened:** The Clean Space has been appointed by professional services firm Forvis Mazars to clean its new two-floor office in central Manchester. The contract covers office, window, carpet and washroom cleaning. According to the report, The Clean Space surveyed the site and studied the floor plans before designing a cleaning programme around how each area will be used.

**My take:** Look at how they won it: they didn't just send a price, they studied the building first. When you quote for an office, walk the site, ask how each room is used, and show the client a plan built for their space. A meeting room used twice a week does not need the same attention as a busy kitchen. That kind of thinking is what makes you look like a professional rather than just another quote.

## South African households are booking fewer, shorter cleans

![Gloved hands holding a sponge and spray bottle (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-24/cleaning-supplies-gloves.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Jonathan Borba on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/close-up-of-cleaning-supplies-with-gloves-28576636/)*

**What happened:** BusinessTech reports that South African households are struggling to afford domestic help as living costs rise and salaries stay flat. Employers are cutting back on cleaning, and a representative of the cleaning platform SweepSouth said clients are booking fewer and shorter sessions and tipping less.

**My take:** When money is tight, clients don't always cancel; they shrink. Be ready with smaller packages: a two-hour "essentials" clean, a fortnightly plan instead of weekly, or a kitchen-and-bathroom-only visit. It is better to keep a client on a smaller plan than to lose them completely. The same pressure is being felt by households in Nigeria, so plan your packages before your clients ask.

Read the full story: [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=884276)

## A US cleaning services company keeps buying smaller firms

![A worker in a safety harness pressure washing a building (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-24/pressure-washing-facade.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Bijou Baby on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/person-in-yellow-helmet-using-a-power-spray-5248733/)*

**What happened:** Crystal Clean, a US industrial and environmental services company, announced that it has bought two more businesses, Insta-Clean and Green Site Services. It has made several acquisitions in recent years. This comes from the company's own press release.

**My take:** Big companies grow by buying smaller ones that already have good clients and good systems. Even if you never plan to sell, build your business as if someone might one day want to buy it: written processes, signed contracts, clean accounts and a team that can work without you. That is what makes a business valuable, whether you sell it or keep it.

Read the announcement: [Crystal Clean on PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/crystal-clean-announces-acquisitions-of-insta-clean-and-green-site-services-302946105.html)

## Quick tip of the week

Black Friday is on 27 November, and many shoppers are already in deal-hunting mode. If you run a cleaning business, think about a simple, honest offer, such as a gift voucher for a deep clean that people can give to family before Christmas. Set a clear end date, and make sure the price still covers your costs.
