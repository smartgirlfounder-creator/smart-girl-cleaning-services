## South Africa looks at protections for cleaners who find work through apps

![A gloved hand wiping a kitchen counter with a sponge (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-26/kitchen-counter-cleaning.webp)
*Illustrative photo: RDNE Stock project on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/a-person-cleaning-a-kitchen-counter-5591908/)*

**What happened:** South Africa's Department of Employment and Labour has published a study on people who get work through apps, including domestic workers who are booked through cleaning platforms and e-hailing drivers. The research, carried out by the University of Cape Town's Development Policy Research Unit, surveyed 139 platform workers, among them 27 domestic workers. It found that many are treated as independent contractors, which can leave them outside the laws on minimum wage, working conditions and labour relations. The department says the findings are not government policy yet, but will feed into future consultations.

**My take:** If your cleaning business uses an app, a WhatsApp group or a booking platform to send cleaners to jobs, watch this closely. The direction of travel around the world is the same: governments want people who clean for a living to have basic protections, whatever their contract is called. Don't wait for the law to force you. Write down how you pay your cleaners, how you handle cancellations and how you keep them safe in clients' homes. A fair, clear system is also how you keep your best people.

## LAWMA starts night-time clean-ups across Lagos

![An overflowing wheelie bin on a street at night (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-26/bin-at-night.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Erik Mclean on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/full-garbage-bins-standing-along-a-sidewalk-at-night-12641841/)*

**What happened:** The Lagos Waste Management Authority (LAWMA) has launched a night-time clean-up operation targeting major waste black spots, starting along the Isheri–Igando–LASU–Iba corridor. The agency's acting managing director, Olajuwon Akinyemi, said LAWMA is also working towards more than 10 operational regions across the state. It comes alongside a 60-day clean-up drive against illegal dumpsites that began on 23 November.

**My take:** Working at night is not new to cleaning, but many small cleaning businesses in Nigeria still only sell daytime hours. Offices, shops, banks and restaurants often prefer cleaners who come after closing or before opening, when nobody is in the way. If you can organise safe transport and supervision for your team, after-hours cleaning can become a premium service. With the festive season coming, now is a good time to offer it.

Read the full story: [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/11/24/lawma-launches-night-time-clean-up-across-lagos-targets-major-waste-black-spots/)

## In Singapore, a new district sends robots to clean up spills

![An autonomous street-sweeping robot outside an office building (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-26/robot-street-sweeper.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Ярослав Сапрыкин on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/autonomous-robot-sweeper-cleaning-city-street-19114196/)*

**What happened:** In Punggol Digital District, a new government-developed area in Singapore, cameras and sensors are linked to a central system that can spot a problem, such as a coffee spill in an office block, and send a cleaning robot to deal with it. AFP reports that the robots are being used for jobs like cleaning and security, partly because Singapore's workforce is ageing.

**My take:** This is where large commercial buildings are heading: problems spotted quickly and fixed quickly. Most of us won't be buying robots any time soon, but we can copy the idea. Give your clients one easy way to report a problem, by WhatsApp or a QR code on the wall, and promise a response time. Speed is a service you can sell today, no robot required.

Read the full story: [Malay Mail (AFP)](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/11/26/not-dystopian-just-very-tidy-singapores-new-district-puts-robots-on-cleaning-duty/244813)

## The ISSA Show ends with robots in the spotlight

![Two cleaners in protective suits vacuuming a tiled floor (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-26/cleaners-vacuuming.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Tima Miroshnichenko on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/cleaners-in-ppe-vacuuming-a-tiled-floor-6195288/)*

**What happened:** ISSA, the global cleaning industry association, has wrapped up its 2026 trade show in Las Vegas. It says more than 500 exhibitors took part, and a new robotics area featured more than 40 companies making robots and self-driving floor cleaners. Next year's show is set for 8 to 11 November 2027. ISSA has also elected John Swigart of Spartan Chemical to lead its board as president in 2027.

**My take:** I have shared several robot stories from this show already, and the message is clear: the big players are investing heavily in machines. Here is the part for small business owners. Robots are good at big, open floors. They are not good at kitchens, bathrooms, corners, clutter and people. That detailed work is where a well-trained team still wins, so make sure your training and your checklists are strong.

## Quick tip of the week

The festive season is booking season. The Maids, a US cleaning franchise, says in its own 2026 Holiday Mess Report that 72% of the Americans it surveyed would pay for a professional cleaner before and after holiday celebrations, and 61% would consider a home-cleaning service as a gift. It's a company survey, so take the numbers with a pinch of salt, but the idea works anywhere: package a "before the guests arrive" clean, an "after the party" clean, and a cleaning gift voucher for Christmas.
