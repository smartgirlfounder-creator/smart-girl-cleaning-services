## Winnipeg cleaners win a new deal just before a strike

![A person mopping a tiled floor (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-28/mopping-tiled-floor.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Tima Miroshnichenko on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/person-cleaning-a-tiled-floor-6197106/)*

**What happened:** Cleaners at the University of Winnipeg in Canada, who are employed by the contractor Dexterra, have signed a new collective agreement. A strike was called off just before the deadline. The Uniter, the university's newspaper, reported on 26 November that the cleaners will earn $18 an hour, up from $17, with vacation pay paid as a separate lump sum.

**My take:** Notice what nearly happened here: the university almost lost its cleaning service over one dollar an hour. If you hold contracts with schools, offices or estates, your service depends completely on your team turning up. Before you price your next contract, work out what your cleaners actually need to earn, and build that into your price from the start. It is much cheaper than losing staff, or the contract, halfway through the year.

Read the full story: [The Uniter](https://theuniterwpg.substack.com/p/janitors-sign-new-collective-agreement)

## The dirt your clients can't see matters most

![Fine particles floating in the air of a sunlit kitchen (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-28/dust-in-sunlight.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Anastasia Shuraeva on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/motes-of-dust-in-sunlight-4122913/)*

**What happened:** Two trade magazines, Cleanfax and Cleaning & Maintenance Management, have reported this week on a session at the ISSA Show about "small particle" cleaning: removing the fine dust and particles we can't see with the naked eye. The message was that evidence-based cleaning methods matter more than stronger chemicals, especially for people with chronic health conditions.

**My take:** This is good news for small cleaning businesses, because method beats money. You don't need expensive chemicals to do a healthier clean. Dust from top to bottom, use damp microfibre cloths instead of dry dusters that push dust into the air, and use a vacuum with a good filter. Then explain this to your clients, especially families with asthma, allergies, babies or elderly relatives. When clients understand why your method is different, they stop comparing you on price alone.

Read the full story: [Cleanfax](https://cleanfax.com/small-particle-cleaning-starts-with-what-you-cant-see/)

## A new US rule on who counts as a contractor

![A person in a suit signing a document at a desk (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-28/signing-contract.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Andrea Piacquadio on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/crop-businessman-signing-contract-in-office-3771097/)*

**What happened:** On 19 November, the US Department of Labor announced a final rule on when a worker can be treated as an independent contractor rather than an employee. According to the employment law firm Littler, it largely follows the rule proposed in February 2026 and uses an "economic reality" test. Lawyers say it may still face legal challenges.

**My take:** Many cleaning businesses, in the US and here in Nigeria, call their cleaners "contractors" while telling them exactly when to work, what to wear and how to clean. Wherever you are, the label on the paper is not what matters; what matters is how the work really happens. If you control the hours, the methods and the supplies, that person probably looks like an employee. Get proper local advice and set up your team correctly before it becomes a problem.

Read the full story: [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/dol-releases-final-rule-regarding-independent-contractor-status)

## New York City puts $2.1 million into rights education for domestic workers

![A brick apartment building with fire escapes in New York (illustrative photo)](/images/articles/2026-11-28/new-york-apartment-building.webp)
*Illustrative photo: Brett A on [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/photo/classic-new-york-brick-building-with-fire-escapes-37278030/)*

**What happened:** On 19 November, the New York City Council announced a $2.1 million investment to help grassroots groups teach domestic workers, delivery workers, street vendors and other "nontraditional" workers about their legal rights and how to report violations.

**My take:** Around the world, domestic workers are learning their rights, and that is a good thing for honest cleaning businesses. The businesses that treat their people fairly have nothing to fear and a lot to gain. Write down your pay rates, hours and rules, give every cleaner a copy, and keep records. A well-informed cleaner who is treated well becomes your best recruiter.

Read the full announcement: [New York City Council](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/11/19/3359/)

## Quick tip of the week

December is almost here, and in Lagos that means "Detty December" and busy short-let apartments. Nairametrics reported in September that the number of short-let units in Lagos keeps growing and that operators are under pressure, with costs like cleaning continuing even when apartments are empty. If you clean in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, approach short-let owners now with a simple, reliable turnover package (clean, change linen, restock, report any damage with photos) before the December rush starts.
