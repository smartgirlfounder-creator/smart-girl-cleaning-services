Your first ten customers are the hardest and the most important. They prove the business works, give you your first reviews and teach you more than any course.

Here is a practical way to find them, without a big budget.

## Start with the people who already trust you

Your first customers are rarely strangers. They are people who already know you: friends, family, former colleagues, neighbours, your religious community, parents at your children's school.

Don't ask them for favours. Tell them clearly what you now offer and who it is for, and ask: "Do you know anyone who needs this?" Referrals from people who trust you are the warmest leads you will ever get.

## Make your offer impossible to misunderstand

Before you tell anyone, write down:

- what exactly you do
- who it is for
- where you work
- what it costs, or how you quote
- how to book

If people have to ask five questions before they understand your offer, most won't ask any.

## Show the work, every day

Post what you do, consistently. Before and after photos, short videos of the process, the problem you solved, what the customer said. You don't need to be polished. You need to be visible and real.

People buy from businesses they have watched for a while.

## Go where your customers already are

Think about where your ideal customer spends time, online and offline. Estate WhatsApp groups. Markets. Churches and mosques. Offices. Local Facebook groups. Show up there with something helpful, not just an advert.

## Make the first job easy to say yes to

A small, clear first offer lowers the risk for a new customer: one room instead of the whole house, a trial session, a smaller package. Deliver it brilliantly, then offer the next step.

## Turn every customer into two more

After every job:

1. Ask how it went, and fix anything that wasn't right.
2. Ask for a short review or a photo you can share.
3. Ask: "Who else do you know who would love this?"

Ten customers who each bring one more is twenty. That's how a business starts to grow on its own.

## Track where every customer came from

Write it down: referral, WhatsApp group, TikTok, walk-in. After ten customers, you will know exactly where to spend your time next.

> You don't have a sales problem. You have a "nobody knows you exist yet" problem. Fix that first.
