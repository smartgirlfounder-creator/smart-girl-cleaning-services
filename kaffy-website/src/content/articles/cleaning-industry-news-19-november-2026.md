## 1. A Sydney cleaner said no to a $1.5 million job

**What happened:** Sara Allan of Housekeeping Heroes, a cleaning company in Sydney, Australia, turned down a pre-handover clean at a block of new townhouses worth around $1.5 million each. She says the pay offered was far too low for the work, and that when buyers find problems in new homes, builders are quick to blame the cleaner. This is her side of the story; the builder's response wasn't reported.

**My take:** Saying no is a business skill. Not every job is worth your name. Before you accept a job, photograph and write down any damage or defects that are already there, and put it in your terms that you are not responsible for them. If the price is too low for you to do the job properly, walk away politely. A cheap job done badly will cost you more in reputation than it pays you.

## 2. Robots and AI won the big awards at the ISSA Show

**What happened:** The ISSA Show North America in Las Vegas is the biggest cleaning trade show of the year. Its 2026 Innovation Award winners, announced on 18 November, included:

- **Gausium's Marvel**, a robot that sweeps and scrubs floors on its own
- **SiteVue AI safety glasses**, which use AI to check in real time that cleaners have completed each step of a procedure
- **Clorox Healthcare disinfecting wipes** with a one-minute contact time
- **Misco's Infinity Hand Care**, which won the Restroom award and the People's Choice award

**My take:** Technology is arriving in commercial cleaning first, and small companies will feel it next. Glasses that check a cleaner's work will divide opinion: some will call it quality control, others will call it surveillance. Either way, the lesson for us is the same: clients increasingly want proof of work. You don't need AI glasses for that. A simple checklist with before-and-after photos does the same job today.

Read the full announcement: [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/18/3406225/0/en/issa-show-north-america-redefines-innovation-with-2026-award-winners-shaping-the-future-of-clean.html)

## 3. A robot built to clean public toilets is on show in Tokyo

**What happened:** Primech Holdings is showing HYTRON, a robot designed to clean toilets, urinals, sinks, floors and mirrors without a person controlling it, at the Building Maintenance & Clean Expo in Tokyo from 18 to 20 November.

**My take:** Robots will take the jobs nobody wants first, and restroom cleaning is top of that list. But a robot won't notice a broken soap dispenser, a leaking pipe or a client who needs something extra. The cleaning businesses that win will be the ones that sell care, attention to detail and trust, things a machine can't offer.

Read more: [Primech press release](https://www.helpareporter.com/news-releases/primech-holdings-nasdaq-pmec-announces-hytron-showcase-at-building)

## 4. Cape Town cleaners went on strike for a 10% raise and settled for 5%

**What happened:** Housekeepers at Nox Cape Town, a company that services rental properties, went on strike for five days asking for a 10% pay rise. They accepted the 5% the company first offered, plus three months' back pay and payment for two of the strike days. Their shop steward, Vivienne Francisco, said: "The 5% increase is not going to help."

**My take:** Pay and keeping good staff are among the biggest headaches for cleaning business owners, in South Africa, in Nigeria and everywhere else. Your cleaners are your product. If they leave, your quality leaves with them. Pay isn't the only thing that keeps people, but it matters. Ask yourself: what are you doing to keep your best cleaners?

Read the full story: [Bizcommunity (originally reported by GroundUp)](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/luxury-rental-cleaners-end-strike-and-accept-5-raise-962814a)

## 5. A client says Instagram-booked cleaners left her gas on

**What happened:** A woman in the Bronx, New York, posted on TikTok that a cleaning company she booked through Instagram left a stove burner on, so her home smelled of gas, and threw away her belongings, including food, a Pyrex bowl and silverware. These are her allegations, as reported by The Mary Sue.

**My take:** This is every client's fear, and it shows exactly what your business can promise instead. Three things every cleaning business should do:

1. Send the same team to each client whenever you can.
2. Finish every job with a closing checklist: stove off, taps off, windows and doors locked.
3. Never throw anything away without asking the client first.

Then tell clients you do this. Trust is a selling point.

Read the full story: [The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/woman-says-she-found-her-purse-dishes-food-and-more-in-the-trash-after-a-cleaning-service-came-over-while-she-was-away/)

## 6. It was World Toilet Day

**What happened:** 19 November was World Toilet Day. This year's UN theme is "Safe toilets. Equal futures.", focusing on sanitation and gender equality.

**My take:** Hygiene is our industry's business. Days like this are a good reason to post bathroom-cleaning tips and remind your clients why a properly cleaned bathroom protects their family's health.

Read more: [United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/observances/toilet-day)

## Quick tip of the week

Experts are warning people in the US not to use their oven's self-clean setting right before Thanksgiving. The door locks for hours, and if the oven breaks, repairs are hard to get during the holidays. If you run a cleaning business, this is a good time to offer a pre-holiday oven and kitchen deep clean, booked at least a week before the big day.
